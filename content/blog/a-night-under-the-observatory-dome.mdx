At 11:40 pm on a clear August night, twenty-two Year 8 girls in hoodies and blankets queued on the roof of the Glasshouse for their turn at the eyepiece. Saturn had just cleared the ridge.

The first girl to look said nothing for a long moment. Then: "It looks fake. It looks exactly like the drawings."

## Why we do it

Astronomy is one of the few sciences where the real thing is genuinely, breathtakingly better than any diagram. Our rooftop observatory lets every girl see the Moon's craters, Jupiter's moons and, when we're lucky, the rings of Saturn with her own eyes.

## The questions

The best part of the night wasn't the telescope. It was the questions. Why are the rings flat? How long would it take to get there? If light takes time to travel, are we seeing Saturn as it was? (Yes — about 80 minutes ago.)

Back in the classroom, those questions became a unit on light, distance and scale that the girls designed themselves.

## Next time

The Glasshouse observatory opens for families on the first Friday of each term. Boarders get monthly sessions, weather permitting — and the monsoon permits rarely.
