Every Year 9 tutor group at Aurelia Hall begins Tuesday and Thursday with the same ritual: the tutor opens a novel and reads aloud for fifteen minutes. No questions, no worksheets, no "what is the author trying to say here". Just a story.

When we started, a few girls rolled their eyes. They were fourteen. Reading aloud was for Pre-Prep.

## What changed

By half-term, the tutor groups had opinions. Strong ones. One group staged a protest when a chapter ended on a cliffhanger. Another started a waiting list for the library copy so they could read ahead (strictly forbidden; widely done).

Reading aloud does something silent reading can't. It lets a whole room share the same rhythm of a sentence. It models how a good reader handles a tricky passage. And it gives girls who find reading hard a way into books they would never have picked up alone.

## The research agrees

Studies of reading aloud to secondary pupils have found gains in comprehension, particularly for weaker readers — and a measurable boost in enjoyment. Enjoyment matters: girls who read for pleasure at fifteen are more likely to keep reading as adults.

## What we're reading this term

Year 9 are halfway through a coming-of-age novel set in a hill station; Year 10 asked for science fiction. Our librarians keep a list of the books each group has loved, and we share it with parents at the end of every term.

> The best part is when the bell goes and someone says "one more page". — Year 9 tutor (sample)
