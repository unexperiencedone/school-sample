Clay doesn't care how clever you are. Centre it badly and it will wobble off the wheel no matter how many A*s you have.

That's exactly why we love teaching it.

## Collapse, then try again

In their first pottery session, most girls lose their first pot within ninety seconds. It folds in on itself, or flies off, or develops a hole you could put your fist through. Our studio rule: you laugh, you scrape it off, you wedge the clay and you try again.

By the fourth or fifth attempt, something shifts. Hands steady. Breathing slows. The pot stands.

## Transferable humility

We hear from teachers across the school that girls who've spent time on the wheel are a little more relaxed about getting things wrong in maths or French. Not because pottery is magic, but because it gives them a physical, memorable experience of improving through failure.

## Open studio

The pottery studio is open three lunchtimes a week and on Saturday mornings for boarders. Families are welcome at the end-of-term exhibition, where everything — even the wobbly ones — goes on display.
